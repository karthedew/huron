Raw hierarchy and access target

Accepted target; implementation pending. Organization → Catalog → Namespace → nested Namespace(s) → Collection. Initially one Organization; namespace nesting has no fixed business-depth cap, subject to strict names and path safety. Empty namespaces persist; every collection belongs to a namespace, and directories within collections are not namespaces.

Administrator (administrator) administers the Organization and all its catalogs. Catalog Owner (catalog_owner), Catalog Contributor (catalog_contributor) and Catalog Reader (catalog_reader) apply only to their assigned catalog and all its descendants. Group grants are additive. Administrators appoint owners; owners manage only Reader/Contributor membership on their own catalog. There are no namespace/collection grants or creator privileges.

The server re-maps token groups on every request; the roles claim and CLI categories confer no authority. See N14 contracts and the migration sequence. Older five-role descriptions below apply to the legacy hierarchy or superseded Raw design, not this target. No new Raw history or versioning is introduced.

RBAC & Authentication

Projects are the data pond and the primary authorization boundary. Roles bind identity-provider groups at stable scopes; roles expand to capabilities; layers add their own restrictions on top.

Explore the current implementation: RBAC UML class atlas — searchable, zoomable diagrams of the role vocabulary, scopes, bindings, the authorizer decision procedure, AccessPolicy, governance administration and persistence, with enforced capabilities distinguished from vocabulary. Identity resolution is in the authentication atlas.

Current source ownership: core.security colocates roles, discovery rules, AccessPolicy, bindings, policies, service-account and authentication workflows. Its CatalogFacts port asks only for authoritative classification, ancestry, existence and organization naming; Metastore projections reuse the visibility-filtered readers. Security does not import Catalog. Stored governance provenance comes from the bound actor; public workflows accept no caller attribution argument. The Core map records the current package graph; the roadmap records migration verification.

1. Principles

  • The project is the pond. Membership, quota, retention defaults, classification, compute limits, cost attribution, and incident blast radius all bound at the project. No per-user storage allocations, no per-dataset authorization domains by default, no global shared raw directory.
  • Organization membership grants discovery of org-public projects — nothing more. Project membership never follows automatically from org membership.
  • Ordinary org members cannot create projects. project.create belongs to the organization admin (and platform owner); there is no separate creator role.
  • Operational access ≠ data access. A platform operator does not automatically read scientific data.
  • Uploading ≠ reading forever. A producer identity can be write-only; raw payload read is its own capability.
  • Lineage does not grant access (invariant L); restricted upstreams render redacted.
  • Prefer IdP groups over direct user bindings. Thousands of user rows are a smell; bind group:mcnp-analysts once.

2. Roles

Huron has five human roles — and no others: the complete vocabulary, matching core.security.roles, the migration seed, and the contract test that diffs the two. There is deliberately no "no role" entry anywhere in the taxonomy or the UI: a principal without a binding is simply unbound — it holds nothing, sees nothing (the 404-not-403 rule), and appears in no role picker. Every picker lists exactly the five roles and nothing else. Service accounts are not part of the role system at all — their single authority is intrinsic (below).

The data-steward role was removed (2026-08-31, deliberate simplification). Any developer creates datasets and commits revisions — dataset creation is no longer a governance act gated on a dedicated role. The governance the steward carried moved to the admins by scope: the project admin owns project-scope acts (Gold publication approval, project schema enrichments, project policies) and the organization admin owns org-scope acts (organization metadata schemas, science profiles, organization policies). What keeps this safe is not a human gate but the inherited policies every dataset is created under, plus validation-gated publication.

Service accounts: no roles, one intrinsic authority

Service accounts hold no roles at all (2026-08-31 — this replaced the earlier pipeline/ingest_pipeline role pair). An SA is a single-purpose identity: it exists to let an external pipeline authenticate and perform CRUD against datasets of its organization at the silver and gold layers. That authority is intrinsic to being a service account — conferred in whole when an organization admin creates it (service_account.manage), revoked in whole when the SA or its keys are revoked — never granted, shaped, or widened through role bindings. Role bindings are for humans; the database refuses a binding whose principal is a service account.

What every service account can do, org-wide and nothing else:

AuthorityScope
dataset.create / dataset.update / dataset.discover every dataset of the owning organization — a feed entering at silver mints the dataset it fills (direct entry), under the same inherited policies as any human creation
data.read + data.write on silver and gold only

No raw, no bronze, in either direction: raw and bronze are the human contribution surface (developers), and the most sensitive material lives in raw. "Delete" in CRUD means the snapshot lifecycle — replacing working branches, region replacement, superseding — never destroying history: purge stays the two-person human flow.

The load-bearing invariant is the principal kind: silver/gold data.write is held by service-account principals exclusively, and now by construction — no role in the vocabulary carries it, so no binding can leak it to a human (invariant J). Humans — admins included — never hold those writes; a human initiates manual intake and a service writes.

Org-wide authority, prefix-narrow credentials. An SA's authorization ceiling is its organization, but the storage credentials it actually receives stay narrow: every write mints a short-lived credential scoped to the one repo prefix of the write-intent (§7), so a compromised job credential still cannot roam the org. Distinct from all of this are Huron's own platform services — the layer intake service, the deferred bronze builder, the canonicalizer, product runner, retention worker — which run under internal deployment identities, not organization service accounts, and are how bronze gets written despite SAs having no bronze access.

Design intent not yet built (unchanged by the simplification): raw payload read as a capability stricter than dataset read — today reader's data.read spans all four layers including raw payload — and classification-gated discovery (§5). Both remain intended; neither has capabilities in the built vocabulary yet.

3. Dataset lifecycle and policy inheritance

Datasets are ACTIVE on creation. A developer creates a dataset and ingests into it immediately; there is no approval gate and no steward checklist. The old DRAFT → ACTIVE activation flow is removed with the steward role — lifecycle is ACTIVE → RETIRED (plus SUPERSEDED for slug mistakes). What replaced the human gate is structural:

  • Creation happens under inherited policy (below): classification default, retention, and required metadata schemas apply from the first revision — the form or client call cannot complete without satisfying the effective schema.
  • Publication is validation-gated, not approval-gated: bronze/silver snapshots publish only when the profile's checks pass; Gold additionally needs the project admin's publication approval.

Policy inheritance: organization → project → dataset

Policies are governed documents attached at a scope with the corresponding capability — organization.policy_attach (organization admin), project.policy_attach (project admin), dataset.policy_attach (admins only, for the rare per-dataset exception). Every dataset inherits its project's policies, and every project inherits its organization's — attaching a policy at a scope is how an admin governs everything below it without touching each dataset:

effective(dataset) = organization policy
                   ⊕ project policy          # nearer scope refines farther
                   ⊕ dataset policy          # rare, admin-attached exception

policy keys and their merge rule:
  classification_default      nearest scope wins
  retention                   nearest scope wins (per category)
  quota                       nearest scope wins; a nearer scope may only tighten
  required_metadata_schemas   COMPOSE — org base + project enrichment, exactly the
                              schema registry's extends chain (catalog-search)
  entry_layers_allowed        nearest scope wins; e.g. an org may forbid silver entry

Composition mirrors the schema registry: where a policy carries schema requirements, the effective schema is the org base composed with the project enrichment, and ingest validates against the composition. Policy attachments are generation-versioned documents like every other descriptive document (Raw §8), audited, and resolved at decision time — a policy change applies to the next act, never retroactively to committed revisions.

5. Capabilities and bindings

Roles expand internally into capabilities, so role definitions can change without rewriting the authorizer:

organization.create      organization.update      organization.delete
organization.policy_attach
project.create           project.update           project.policy_attach
dataset.create           dataset.update           dataset.policy_attach
dataset.discover
rbac.binding_manage      service_account.manage   user.manage
data.read {layer…}       data.write {layer…}      # the two layer-scoped capabilities

Sixteen capabilities total; data.read/data.write carry the layer dimension as Binding.layers and everything else is control-plane. Retention, purge and audit acts are guarded by the admin capabilities on their scope today; splitting them into finer capabilities is a future refinement, not a v1 need.

Bindings live in role_bindings (scope SYSTEM | ORGANIZATION | PROJECT | DATASET) with conditions and expiry:

{"principal": "group:mcnp-analysts", "role_code": "developer",
 "scope_type": "PROJECT", "scope_id": "0191f0c3-7c2a-7f31-9a4e-2b6d5c1e4a70",
 "effect": "ALLOW", "expires_at": "2027-01-01T00:00:00Z",
 "conditions": {"maximum_classification": "internal"}}

Resolution order: explicit deny → dataset binding → project binding → organization binding → platform default (deny). Inheritance flows downward; dataset-level bindings and layer restrictions only ever narrow. Listing is authorization: visible_project_ids is applied in SQL before pagination, never as a post-filter; where existence is sensitive the API returns 404, not 403. Role bindings are never cached beyond the request.

Classification gating

Classification always gates data access (payload, arrays). Whether it also gates discovery is per-code deployment configuration:

classification:
  values:
    - {code: internal,     order: 10, discoverable: true}
    - {code: confidential, order: 20, discoverable: true}    # metadata visible, data gated
    - {code: restricted,   order: 30, discoverable: false}   # existence itself is gated

Non-discoverable classifications are excluded from browse, search, counts, and lineage rendering for principals without clearance — enforced in the same visible_project_ids-style predicate, so it composes with pagination correctly. This is written into the authorizer from day one, because retrofitting discovery gating is an authorization-model change, not a filter.

API keys

  • Format hk_{key_id}_{secret}; only sha256(secret) stored; the secret is shown once at creation. Lookup by key_id, constant-time compare.
  • Self-service: any user creates/lists/revokes their own keys (apikey.manage_own) via UI or API; owners and admins can revoke within their scope; all lifecycle events audited.
  • A key is a subset of its owner: scopes (capability groups such as read, ingest, publish) and optional project restriction narrow the owner's live permissions; a key never outlives or exceeds them, and revoking the user kills the keys.
  • Expiry required: default 90 days, maximum 400; last_used_at tracked; unused-key reports for owners.
  • Service accounts are first-class principals owned by an organization (created and revoked by its org admin), roleless by design (§2); their keys are managed by the org admin and carry the SA's intrinsic scope, never more.

7. Data-plane enforcement

Catalog RBAC is ineffective if the storage underneath is world-browsable. Preferred design: users submit dataset IDs, not paths — user → API → authorization → scoped access — and the storage layers enforce defense in depth:

PlaneEnforcement
NFS (raw) The raw filesystem is mounted by Huron services only and is not exported to user machines. Raw payload reaches users exclusively through audited API download capabilities (issued per file, re-authorized at issue time — discovery ≠ download). If site operations require direct mounts anyway: per-project POSIX groups, separate roots per security domain, no world-readable parents, read-only exports of published trees, writes only into API-managed landing locations. Sealing (0444/0555) backstops immutability either way.
Object storage (derived) No standing user credentials. Reads happen via short-lived, prefix-scoped STS credentials from the grant flow (API §5); TTL ≤ 1 h, renewable while authorization still holds; scope is exactly one repo prefix, read-only. Pipeline writes use per-job credentials scoped to the repo being built. Bucket policy denies everything else; only the projection worker writes {prefix}-index.
Polaris Grants derived one-way from Huron bindings; non-discoverable/high-classification products never sync (see Polaris §3).

8. Recommended default policy

platform_owner        → creates organizations; provisions users
organization_admin    → creates projects; org policies (schemas, retention defaults,
                        classification); service accounts
project_admin         → membership and bindings; project policies (enrichment schemas,
                        retention, quota); Gold publication approval
developer             → creates datasets (ACTIVE immediately); commits raw revisions;
                        manual bronze intake; submits candidate products
reader                → discovers and reads what its scope allows
service accounts (roleless, org-scoped)       → the only silver/gold writers; dataset CRUD
platform service identities (internal)        → bronze builds/intake, retention, projection

The load-bearing defaults: org members don't create projects; project membership is explicit; datasets are created under inherited policy and are active immediately; committed Raw files are overwritten only by a Developer's audited overwrite commit (§4); only services write published Bronze/Silver/Gold; the project is the pond. Roles stack — a person can hold developer on one project and project_admin on another; bind IdP groups, not individuals, wherever possible.
